Imagine booking a facial before a wedding, then discovering that the treatment uses a blade and leaves your skin more sensitive for a short time. Knowing what a dermaplane treatment involves helps you ask better questions and avoid booking based on vague promises.

*Last reviewed: 20 October 2026.* This guide provides general information, not a diagnosis or personal medical advice. A qualified treating professional must assess your skin and medical history.

## What Is a Dermaplane Treatment?

A dermaplane treatment is a professional exfoliation procedure that uses a sterile blade or specialised instrument to remove surface dead skin cells and fine facial hair. The instrument moves across prepared skin with controlled strokes. Some people seek it for facial hair removal or because they want to discuss a temporary change in how their skin looks and feels.

Dermaplaning cuts hair at the skin surface. It doesn’t permanently remove hair, change its growth pattern, or guarantee lighter skin. Regrowth can feel different because the cut end feels blunt, but the hair itself hasn’t become thicker. Harvard Health describes dermaplaning tools as forms of razors or scalpels and notes the risk of small nicks, especially when technique or hygiene is poor.

A dermaplaning treatment also differs from surgical dermabrasion. Dermabrasion uses controlled surgical scraping to treat selected skin irregularities and requires a different level of assessment and aftercare. Ask the clinic which procedure it means by “dermaplaning” before you pay a deposit.

Dermaplaning removes surface cells and fine hair; it cannot promise permanent hair removal or a guaranteed change in skin colour.

## What Happens During a Dermaplane Facial?

A dermaplane facial usually begins with a consultation and skin assessment. The practitioner should ask about active skin problems, medicines, recent procedures, allergies, and your reason for seeking treatment. Procedures, products, appointment length, and suitability differ by provider.

A typical appointment may include cleansing, skin preparation, controlled surface exfoliation, removal of residue, and written aftercare guidance. The practitioner may work in sections, keeping the skin steady while moving the instrument across the surface. Do not assume every clinic uses the same blade, angle, product, or cleaning process.

During the treatment, you may notice light scraping or brief sensitivity rather than deep pain. Afterwards, the skin can feel temporarily tender, tight, dry, or more reactive. Possible perceived effects such as a smoother-feeling surface should be discussed as possible outcomes, not promised results.

For example, someone with an event on Saturday should ask about recovery and sun exposure before scheduling a Thursday appointment. The clinic may recommend a different date after assessing the person’s skin.

## Who May Not Be Suitable for Dermaplaning?

Active skin problems can increase irritation or complicate healing, so disclose them before any dermaplane treatment. Tell the treating professional about active rashes, open wounds, skin infections, inflamed acne, cold sores, eczema, rosacea, or unexplained sensitivity. Harvard Health specifically advises caution for people with sensitive skin or conditions such as rosacea, eczema, or acne.

Also mention recent [chemical peels](/guides/chemical-peel-treatment-malaysia), laser procedures, injections, surgery, waxing, or other exfoliation. Your medicines and skincare products matter too. Discuss prescription retinoids, acne medicines, medicines that affect healing or bleeding, and any change in your routine. The professional may postpone treatment or recommend an assessment first.

Use this boundary rule: postpone booking until a qualified professional reviews your situation if your skin is broken, inflamed, infected, recently treated, or affected by a condition you cannot identify.

- Describe the current skin concern and when it started.
- List recent procedures and products, including prescription treatments.
- Ask whether the proposed instrument and technique suit your skin.
- Request a professional assessment if the answer remains uncertain.

Clear disclosure before treatment gives a practitioner the information needed to decide whether dermaplaning should proceed.

## Possible Side Effects and Aftercare

Temporary redness, sensitivity, dryness, irritation, and small nicks can occur. Risk depends on the technique, instrument, skin condition, hygiene, and aftercare. A clean, new blade or properly processed equipment reduces avoidable contamination risks, but no procedure removes every risk.

Follow the clinic’s written instructions rather than copying a routine from social media. Protect treated skin from sun exposure, and ask which products to pause and when to restart them. Harvard Health advises avoiding acid- or retinol-based products for a few days after dermaplaning to reduce irritation, but your treating professional should set guidance for your skin.

Contact the clinic if redness, pain, swelling, irritation, or another symptom becomes severe, keeps worsening, or differs from the aftercare instructions. Seek suitable medical attention for urgent symptoms. Don’t scratch, pick, or add new active products while waiting for advice.

## Questions to Ask Before Booking a Dermaplane Facial

A short call or consultation should produce clear answers, not a sales pitch. Write down the responses so you can compare providers consistently.

1. Who will perform the dermaplane facial?
2. What instrument will they use, and is it single-use or sterilised between patients?
3. Am I suitable based on my current skin condition?
4. What risks and short-term reactions should I expect?
5. Which aftercare steps and product restrictions apply?
6. What does the quoted price include?
7. What should I do if I develop a concerning reaction?

Prices, appointment availability, treatment protocols, and included products differ between clinics. A low quote cannot establish suitability or hygiene. Ask for the full cost before confirming, including any consultation or follow-up charge.
