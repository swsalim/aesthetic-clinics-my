Laser treatment for pimple scars is a plan for marks left after acne, not a single machine. Clinics may describe the same visit as laser treatment scar removal, scar removal laser treatment or an acne scar laser. The useful first step is naming the scar. A narrow pit, a box-shaped dent, a rolling dent and a raised scar need different assessments. The plan also depends on skin tone, and on whether acne is still active.

**Quick answer:** Ask which scar types you have, which laser approach is proposed and why, what else could be combined or used instead, how pigment risk applies to your skin, and how the doctor will decide on another visit. This is general information, not a treatment recommendation. See a registered doctor before you book.

## Acne scar types

Doctors often group facial acne scars by shape. The labels below are a vocabulary for the consultation. They are not a diagnosis you can make from a mirror.

**Icepick scars** are narrow and deep, like a small puncture. A treatment that only brushes the surface can leave the pit in place. Doctors sometimes discuss procedures aimed at the pit itself, alone or with a laser used for the surrounding texture.

**Boxcar scars** are wider depressions with steeper edges. Fractional resurfacing is one approach clinics discuss, because the goal is often to soften the edge and the depth rather than to close a tiny channel.

**Rolling scars** are broad, shallow waves. The skin can look tethered. A laser may be discussed for texture, and a doctor may also discuss releasing the tether or adding volume. Those are different procedures.

**Hypertrophic scars** are raised. They sit in a different category from the dents above. A laser chosen for a depression is not automatically suitable for a raised scar. Keloid scars, which can grow beyond the original spot, need their own assessment. Tell the doctor if scars in your family or on your body tend to stay raised.

Active acne, cysts and picking change the plan. Treating a scar while the skin is still inflamed can be the wrong order. A doctor should say whether the acne needs care before any scar procedure.

## Laser approaches clinics commonly use

The wider map of devices is in [laser treatment in Malaysia](/guides/laser-treatment-malaysia). For scars, these are the groups you will hear most often.

**Fractional lasers**, ablative or non-ablative, are commonly discussed for boxcar and rolling texture. Ablative fractional treatment removes tiny columns of skin and usually involves more peeling and downtime. Non-ablative fractional treatment heats columns without removing the surface and may need a different expectation of change per visit.

**Fully ablative resurfacing** treats a field of skin rather than columns. It is a more intensive conversation, with more downtime and a serious pigment and scarring risk if the settings or the aftercare are wrong. It is not a default for every pimple scar.

**Pigment lasers** such as pico or Q-switched devices are aimed at colour. They are a separate discussion from a laser aimed at a dent. A brown mark left beside a scar may be post-inflammatory pigment, melasma or another condition. [Melasma treatment in Malaysia](/guides/melasma-treatment-malaysia) explains why that colour should be identified before a laser.

Ask the doctor to point at each scar and say which approach is for that scar. A single "scar removal laser" line on a menu does not do that.

## Alternatives and combinations

Laser treatment is one option. A doctor may suggest something else, or a combination, after looking at the scars.

**Microneedling** uses fine needles to make controlled punctures. It is not a laser. [Microneedling treatment in Malaysia](/guides/microneedling-treatment-malaysia) covers what to ask, including pigment risk. Some clinics discuss needling that also delivers heat. Ask which of those you are being offered.

**Chemical peels** apply a solution at a chosen depth. They are sometimes discussed for shallow colour and texture, not as a substitute for treating a deep icepick scar. [Chemical peel treatment in Malaysia](/guides/chemical-peel-treatment-malaysia) is about comparing the doctor, the formulation and the fee.

**Subcision** uses a needle under the skin to release a scar that is tethered down. It is a procedure with its own bruising, swelling and other risks. Doctors sometimes pair it with a laser or with filler for selected rolling scars. Ask who performs it and how complications are handled.

**Fillers** can be discussed to lift some rolling scars for a period of time. Fillers are temporary, and injections carry risks, including injury to a blood vessel. Read [dermal filler injections in Malaysia](/guides/dermal-filler-injections-malaysia) before you treat filler as a small add-on.

A combination can be reasonable. It can also be a way to sell several procedures before the first one has been reviewed. Ask what each step is for, the order, and what you would do if you only did one of them.

## Sessions and expectations

Scar treatment is typically more than one visit. Skin is reviewed between visits, and the next step depends on how it healed. This page does not state a session count or a fee, because both vary by scar, device, area and clinic, and a published number would go out of date.

Ask how the doctor will judge improvement, what "better" means for your scars, and when they would stop. A package sold before that examination is a price structure, not a clinical plan. Photographs taken in the same light can help you and the doctor look at change. They do not prove what your skin will do.

## Risks, especially on darker skin

Any scar laser can redden, swell, hurt, blister, infect or scar. It can also leave skin darker or lighter than the surrounding face.

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation is the extra colour skin can produce after it has been inflamed. It matters for laser treatment scar removal on darker skin tones, including many Asian and Malaysian skin tones. A strong setting, sun exposure after treatment, or picking the skin can add to that risk. Lighter patches are also possible and can be as visible as the original scar.

Ask whether the doctor treats patients with your skin tone, whether a test spot is appropriate, and what the plan is if colour changes. Sunscreen and the rest of the aftercare are part of the risk discussion, not an optional extra. If you already have melasma or widespread dark patches, say so before a scar laser is booked.

## Aftercare

Use the aftercare sheet from the clinic that treated you. Typical instructions include gentle cleansing, a moisturiser the doctor has agreed, strict sun protection, and a pause on exfoliants, peels and new active products until the skin has settled. Avoid picking flakes and avoid heat, heavy exercise and makeup for as long as the doctor advises for that device.

Call the clinic if pain increases, blisters spread, the area weeps, redness spreads, or a dark or light patch appears and worries you. Do not book the next session over skin that has not healed. The general downtime notes in [laser treatment in Malaysia](/guides/laser-treatment-malaysia) explain why ablative and non-ablative visits cannot share one recovery timetable.

## How to choose a qualified doctor

Start with the person, then the device.

- Who examines the scars, and who performs each procedure if a combination is proposed?
- Is that doctor on the [Malaysian Medical Council](https://mmc.gov.my) register?
- Does a current LCP, the Letter of Credentialing and Privileging, name the laser or other procedure? Which chapter of the [Ministry of Health registry](https://www.moh.gov.my/en/core/health-information/national-registry-of-rmp-practising-aesthetic-medical-practice) lists them?
- Which scar types do they see on your face, and which procedure is for each?
- What pigment risk applies to your skin, and what is the downtime for this device?
- What are the alternatives, including doing less?
- What is the fee for consultation, each session, review and care of a complication?
