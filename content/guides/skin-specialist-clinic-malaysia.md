A search for a **clinic skin specialist** in Malaysia is usually a search for a doctor who can assess a skin disease. Clinics also use that phrase, and "specialist skin clinic", for rooms that mainly sell peels, lasers, and injectables. The words on the glass do not tell you which one you have found.

This guide separates a dermatologist from a general practitioner and from an aesthetic doctor, explains when a medical skin problem and a cosmetic visit go to different places, and shows how to check the registers before you book. It does not diagnose a rash or a mole, and it does not rank clinics.

**Quick answer:** A doctor may practise as a specialist in Malaysia only if their name is on the Specialist Register for that specialty. Dermatology is one of the specialties the Malaysian Medical Council lists. Search the doctor's full name on the [National Specialist Register](https://www.nsr.org.my/list11.asp), confirm medical registration on the [Malaysian Medical Council MeRITS search](https://merits.mmc.gov.my/search), and ask the clinic who will see you. If the visit is a cosmetic procedure, check the aesthetic letter as well.

## What a Skin Specialist Means in Malaysia

People say skin specialist, consultant dermatologist, and pakar kulit for the doctor they want when a skin problem needs a specialist opinion. The legal category is narrower than the phrase. The [National Specialist Register's about page](https://www.nsr.org.my/About-NSR.html) describes that register as the Malaysian Medical Council's database of specialist medical practitioners. It says only medical practitioners on the Specialist Register can practise in their registered specialty.

The Council's [Procedures and Guidelines for Specialist Registration](https://mmc.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/THE-PROCEDURES-AND-GUIDELINES-FOR-SPECIALIST-REGISTRATION-version-After-MPM-459-Meeting.pdf) reproduce section 14A of the Medical Act 1971: no person whose name has not been entered into the Register shall practise as a specialist in that specialty. The same document says the Medical (Amendment) Act 2024 and the Medical (Amendment) Regulations 2025 took effect on 1 July 2025, so use the current Council text rather than an old fee table or validity period.

[Dermatology](https://mmc.gov.my/list-of-specialties-and-fields-of-practice/) is listed under internal medicine. Paediatric dermatology is listed separately, under general paediatrics. The public [specialist search](https://www.nsr.org.my/list11.asp) offers both as filters, including a Kuala Lumpur state filter, and the [NSR FAQ](https://www.nsr.org.my/FAQ.html) says the site is free for the public.

"Consultant" is a title hospitals and clinics use. The 2016 [Dermatology Services Operational Policy](https://www.moh.gov.my/images/04-penerbitan/polisi/7.pdf) uses both specialist and consultant for roles inside Ministry hospital dermatology services. A website line that says consultant dermatologist still has to match a register entry.

Specialist registration sits on top of ordinary medical registration. Section 14B, as reproduced in the Council's procedures, starts from full registration under section 14. The Council's [specialist-registration FAQ](https://mmc.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/FREQUENTLY-ASKED-QUESTIONS-FAQ-ENTRY-INTO-THE-SPECIALIST-REGISTER-SR-OF-THE-MMC.pdf) says a registered medical practitioner who wishes to practise as a specialist must be on the Specialist Register. The NSR FAQ says that entry does not replace the Annual Practising Certificate, the yearly licence to practise as a medical practitioner.

## Dermatologist, GP, or Aesthetic Doctor

These three labels get mixed together on clinic websites. They are not three names for the same credential.

A dermatologist, for this check, is a doctor whose specialist entry is in dermatology, or in paediatric dermatology when that is the claim. The [Council's specialist registration page](https://mmc.gov.my/specialist-registration-sr/) says only medical practitioners on the Specialist Register can practise in their specialty.

A general practitioner is a registered medical practitioner. That fact alone does not put them on the Specialist Register for dermatology. [How to check a registered medical practitioner in Malaysia](/guides/check-registered-medical-practitioner-malaysia) walks through the MeRITS search. Keep that check when a headline sounds like a specialty.

An aesthetic doctor is a description clinics use. It is not a specialty on the Council's list. The Ministry of Health Malaysia's [Guidelines on Aesthetic Medical Practice for Registered Medical Practitioners](https://hq.moh.gov.my/medicalprac/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Guidelines_on_Aesthetic_Medical_Practice_for_Registered_Medical_Practitioners_2nd_edition.pdf) (second edition, 2020) split aesthetic practice by chapter. Chapter 1 says general practitioners are only allowed to perform the non-invasive and minimally invasive procedures listed there. Chapter 2 covers medical specialists. [How to compare LCP-certified aesthetic clinics](/guides/how-to-compare-lcp-certified-aesthetic-clinics) explains the letter and the three public registry lists, so this guide does not repeat the procedure tables.

Chapter 2 says a doctor who intends to practise aesthetic medicine must be fully registered with the Council, hold a current Annual Practising Certificate, and either hold a higher qualification in dermatology with full dermatological training or be on the National Specialist Register in a medical field. They must also obtain a Letter of Credentialing and Privileging for the aesthetic procedures they intend to perform. The chapter treats dermatologists as core medical specialists for that work, and it treats specialists whose usual field is unrelated to dermatology differently. A dermatology entry and an aesthetic letter answer different questions.

| Practitioner | What you are confirming | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dermatologist | Specialist Register entry in dermatology, plus MMC registration and a current APC | [NSR specialist search](https://www.nsr.org.my/list11.asp) and [MeRITS](https://merits.mmc.gov.my/search) |
| General practitioner | MMC registration. An LCP only if an aesthetic procedure is proposed | MeRITS, then the [LCP guide](/guides/how-to-compare-lcp-certified-aesthetic-clinics) |
| Aesthetic doctor, as a clinic label | The doctor's full name. The label itself is not a register category | The clinic, then the same MMC and NSR searches |

## When to See a Skin Specialist Clinic

Use the reason for the visit, not the font on the shopfront.

A specialist skin clinic is the right question when the problem is a medical skin disease. The Ministry's 2016 dermatology operational policy describes hospital dermatology outpatient services as care for skin diseases, with assessment, treatment, and follow-up. Conditions people commonly take to a dermatologist include eczema, psoriasis, acne that is severe or not settling, and a mole that is new, changing, or bleeding. This page cannot tell you which of those you have. If a spot is changing quickly, or a rash comes with feeling unwell, contact a doctor or urgent care rather than booking a cosmetic facial and waiting.

A cosmetic visit is a different question. The aesthetic guideline treats peels, lasers, botulinum toxin, and filler as procedure categories with their own training and letter rules. Use the matching guide for the comparison questions:

- [Chemical peel treatment in Malaysia](/guides/chemical-peel-treatment-malaysia)
- [Laser treatment in Malaysia](/guides/laser-treatment-malaysia)
- [Acne scar laser treatment](/guides/acne-scar-laser-treatment)
- [Melasma treatment in Malaysia](/guides/melasma-treatment-malaysia)
- [How to compare botox clinics](/guides/botox-clinics-how-to-compare-them)
- [Dermal filler injections in Malaysia](/guides/dermal-filler-injections-malaysia)

A dermatologist may offer some of those procedures. Chapter 2 still requires the letter for the aesthetic procedure. If the clinic's answer is only "our doctor is a skin specialist", ask which register entry they mean and which procedures the letter names.

| Reason for the visit | Where to start | What else to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eczema, psoriasis, severe acne, or a changing mole | A doctor registered in dermatology | The referral rule if the clinic is a government hospital service |
| A peel, laser, filler, or botulinum toxin | The named doctor and the LCP for that procedure | Whether that doctor is also on the Specialist Register, if the clinic claims a dermatologist |
| A salon facial with no medical procedure | Outside this directory | The [finding guide](/guides/the-complete-guide-to-finding-an-aesthetic-clinic-in-malaysia) explains why a beauty salon is a different shortlist |

## How to Verify the Doctor

Match one named doctor to one register entry before you pay a booking deposit.

1. Copy the doctor's full name from the clinic page, including the spelling the clinic uses. If the page shows only a first name, ask for the registered name.
2. Open the [NSR specialist search](https://www.nsr.org.my/list11.asp). Search the name. If several people match, compare the specialty and the place of practice shown on the result. Look for dermatology, or paediatric dermatology when the clinic is making that claim.
3. Search the same name on [MeRITS](https://merits.mmc.gov.my/search). [The registration guide](/guides/check-registered-medical-practitioner-malaysia) explains how to read that result. Similar names are common. Do not treat a near match as the same person.
4. Ask the clinic for the current Annual Practising Certificate arrangement. The NSR FAQ is explicit that specialist registration is not the APC.
5. If anyone has proposed an aesthetic procedure, ask to see the Letter of Credentialing and Privileging and compare it with the [Ministry registry chapters](/guides/how-to-compare-lcp-certified-aesthetic-clinics).

Keep the result for your own notes. Clinic pages change, so repeat the search if time has passed. A profile or a "board certified" badge is a prompt to do these steps. It is not the register.

## Referral: Government Care and Private Clinics

There is no single booking rule for every skin clinic in the country. The published rules that do exist are about particular services.

The [Dermatology Services Operational Policy](https://www.moh.gov.my/images/04-penerbitan/polisi/7.pdf), published by the Medical Development Division in July 2016 (MOH/P/PAK/317.16(BP)), says that for general dermatology outpatient services in that policy, patients have to be referred by doctors and self-referral is not accepted. A patient who arrives with a referral is first assessed for urgency. The policy says urgent cases are seen immediately or early, and non-urgent cases are given an appointment within fourteen working days. In hospitals with a resident dermatologist, it says all new cases must be reviewed by the specialist at least once. That document is an operational policy for Ministry hospital dermatology services. Confirm the current path with the hospital you plan to attend, because a 2016 policy is not a live queue.

One hospital publishes its own version of that rule. The [dermatology department at Hospital Sultan Ismail](https://jknjohor.moh.gov.my/hsi/departments-and-units-2/department-8/), Johor Bahru, says only referred cases are accepted, that referrals must come from medical officers in government or private clinics or hospitals, and that cases also need an appointment with the dermatology clinic. A referral can therefore start with a government doctor or a private doctor. It does not have to be a particular brand of clinic. Ask the hospital which letter it will accept.

A private specialist skin clinic is not covered by that hospital policy. Ask the clinic whether the first visit needs a referral letter, which doctor will see you, and whether the quoted amount is a consultation fee. If the clinic cannot answer those three, pause.

## What to Expect at a First Visit

For a Ministry hospital dermatology clinic, bring the referral letter, a list of medicines and creams you already use, and the referring doctor's name. The 2016 policy already describes the urgency sort and the specialist review of new cases. The hospital counter will say where to register.

At a private clinic, ask before you go:

- Which doctor will examine me, and is that person on the Specialist Register for dermatology?
- Is this visit a consultation, a procedure, or both?
- What should I bring, and should I stop any cream beforehand?
- What is included in the fee you quoted?
- If you recommend an aesthetic procedure, which LCP covers it, and what are the alternatives?

Write the answers down. A clear first visit explains the assessment and the choices. It does not promise that a rash will clear, or that a cosmetic result will match a photograph.
