An LCP-certified aesthetic clinic is only as clear as the letter behind the sign. LCP means Letter of Credentialing and Privileging. In Malaysia the Medical Practice Division of the Ministry of Health issues that letter to a registered medical practitioner for aesthetic medical practice. Comparing clinics means comparing those letters, the procedures they name, and the doctors who hold them.

The rules sit in the [Guidelines on Aesthetic Medical Practice for Registered Medical Practitioners](https://hq.moh.gov.my/medicalprac/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Guidelines_on_Aesthetic_Medical_Practice_for_Registered_Medical_Practitioners_2nd_edition.pdf), second edition, published by the Medical Practice Division in 2020. This page follows that document. It is not a substitute for reading it, and it is not medical advice.

## What the letter actually says

The guideline says the Medical Practice Division issues the LCP on the recommendation of the Main Credentialing and Privileging Committee of Aesthetic Medical Practice, after the doctor has completed the required training and assessment. With that letter, the doctor's name is listed in the National Registry of Registered Medical Practitioners Practising Aesthetic Medical Practice.

The registry has three chapters:

- **Chapter 1**, general practitioners.
- **Chapter 2**, medical specialists.
- **Chapter 3**, surgical specialists.

The Ministry publishes the current files on its [national registry page](https://www.moh.gov.my/en/core/health-information/national-registry-of-rmp-practising-aesthetic-medical-practice). Open the chapter that matches the doctor you are asking about. A name in a different chapter, or on an old download, is not the check.

Validity differs by chapter in the 2020 text. A Chapter 1 letter is valid for three years and can be renewed. Chapter 2 and Chapter 3 letters are valid for five years and can be renewed. Ask to see the expiry date. A lapsed letter is a reason to pause, not a detail to tidy up after you have paid a deposit.

The letter is also specific. The general chapter says a practitioner needs an LCP for the aesthetic procedure they intend to perform. Specialist chapters say the letter states the procedures approved. When a clinic says "our doctors are LCP certified", ask for the name on the letter and the procedures printed on it. Write both down.

## The scope behind each chapter

Chapter 1 says general practitioners may perform non-invasive and minimally invasive procedures only, and it lists them. Non-invasive examples in that chapter are superficial chemical peels, microdermabrasion, and intense pulsed light. Minimally invasive examples include medium-depth chemical peels, botulinum toxin injection, filler injection excluding silicone and fat, certain skin-tightening devices limited to the upper dermis, superficial sclerotherapy, and listed laser uses such as pigmentation, benign skin lesions, rejuvenation, and hair removal. The chapter table is the list to ask a GP about. If the treatment you want is not on it, ask which chapter and which doctor is credentialed for that procedure.

The guideline defines the three procedure groups in plain physical terms. Non-invasive means an external application with no break in the skin, aimed at the epidermis. Minimally invasive means a procedure that crosses the skin but stays within the limits the guideline describes. Invasive means a procedure that goes further, often with more extensive tissue involvement. Clinics use softer marketing words. Ask which of the three groups the planned procedure falls into, using the guideline's definitions.

Chapter 2 applies to medical specialists. The guideline requires full registration with the [Malaysian Medical Council](https://mmc.gov.my), a current Annual Practising Certificate, and either a higher qualification in dermatology with full dermatological training or registration on the National Specialist Register in a medical field. Chapter 3 applies to surgical specialists, including the pathway described for plastic surgeons. You do not need to memorise the training modules. You do need the doctor's name, the chapter, and the procedures on the letter.

The Medical Practice Division's [aesthetic practice page](https://hq.moh.gov.my/medicalprac/estetik/) tells the public to check the procedure category and the practitioner: a registered medical practitioner who holds an LCP, confirmed against the MMC register. Do those two checks before you compare anything else.

## A comparison you can repeat

Use one row per clinic. Same columns every time.

1. Clinic name, area, and the page you found it on. [Kuala Lumpur](/kuala-lumpur), [Selangor](/selangor), [Bangsar](/kuala-lumpur/bangsar), [Bukit Bintang](/kuala-lumpur/bukit-bintang), [Cheras](/kuala-lumpur/cheras), [Petaling Jaya](/selangor/petaling-jaya), [Subang Jaya](/selangor/subang-jaya), and [Shah Alam](/selangor/shah-alam) are separate lists. A clinic in Petaling Jaya will not appear on the Kuala Lumpur page.
2. The doctor who will see you, their MMC registration number, and whether the MMC register shows a current practising certificate. [How to check a registered medical practitioner in Malaysia](/guides/check-registered-medical-practitioner-malaysia) covers the register search.
3. LCP chapter, procedures named, and expiry. Match the procedure you are asking about, not a neighbouring one.
4. The premises. The guideline expects aesthetic medical services in a facility licensed or registered under the Private Healthcare Facilities and Services Act 1998 and its regulations. Ask which licence the premises holds and whose name is on it.
5. A written quote. Ask what changes the fee: the procedure, who performs it, the product, the number of areas, and whether a review is included. This guide does not publish prices. The clinic's quote is the figure that counts.
6. What the consultation covers before any procedure is booked: the doctor's explanation of the procedure category, and the consent they ask you to sign. The guideline tells practitioners to communicate risks and obtain valid consent. You are listening for a specific conversation about your plan, not a general brochure.

If two clinics both produce a current letter for the same procedure, you still have a comparison: who will be in the room, what the quote includes, and whether you can travel to that clinic for a review. If only one clinic can produce the letter, the comparison is over.

## How to use the doctor and clinic pages

Start with [aesthetic doctors](/doctors) if you already have a name, or with [doctors in Kuala Lumpur](/kuala-lumpur/doctors) and [doctors in Selangor](/selangor/doctors) if you are choosing by area. Open the profile, note the clinic, then call and ask for the LCP details before you reserve a procedure slot.

A listing on aestheticclinics.my is a way to find clinics and doctors by state and area. It shows contact details and, where the clinic has provided them, services and reviews. It is not a statement that this site has checked the LCP. Treat every profile as a lead. The registry and the letter are the check.

When a profile names more than one clinic, ask which premises you will attend and which doctor will be there that day. An LCP belongs to the practitioner, not to the brand on the door. A group with several branches can have different doctors, and those doctors can hold different letters.

## What to record after you call

Make the notes while you are still on the phone or in the email thread. A later summary from memory drops the expiry date and keeps the slogan.

Write the date of the conversation and the name of the person who answered. Then copy, rather than paraphrase, the procedure name they used. If they said "full face laser", ask which laser and which row of the guideline they mean. If they said "filler", ask which product, and remember the guideline's GP exclusion of silicone and fat. You are not choosing a product from this page. You are making the clinic name the one they intend to use, so a second clinic can be asked about the same thing.

Ask what the written quote will itemise. A usable quote names the procedure, the doctor, the product if there is one, the areas included, the review visit if there is one, and anything the fee does not include. Clinics set their own fees. Nothing on aestheticclinics.my is a price list, and this guide will not invent one. If a clinic offers a package of several procedures, ask for each procedure on its own line with the doctor credentialed for that line. A package price that hides who does what is not comparable.

Then check the premises against the address on the area page. [Mont Kiara](/kuala-lumpur/mont-kiara), [Sri Hartamas](/kuala-lumpur/sri-hartamas), [Subang Jaya](/selangor/subang-jaya), and [Klang](/selangor/klang) are far enough apart that a review visit at a different branch is a different plan. Ask "if I need to be seen again, which door do I come back to, and which doctor is there?" Write the answer next to the quote.

It helps to keep the public sources next to the notes: the [2020 guideline PDF](https://hq.moh.gov.my/medicalprac/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Guidelines_on_Aesthetic_Medical_Practice_for_Registered_Medical_Practitioners_2nd_edition.pdf), the [national registry](https://www.moh.gov.my/en/core/health-information/national-registry-of-rmp-practising-aesthetic-medical-practice), and the [MMC site](https://mmc.gov.my). You are matching three documents to one doctor. When they match, you can compare waiting time, travel, and the quote. When they do not match, travel and the quote are irrelevant.

## When the paperwork does not match

Stop if any of these are true, and ask again in writing:

- The person named in the advertisement is not the person on the LCP.
- The procedure you want is not on the letter, and the clinic cannot name the doctor whose letter does include it.
- The expiry date has passed.
- The MMC register does not show the doctor, or does not show a valid practising certificate.
- The clinic will only describe the doctor as "certified" and will not show the chapter or the procedures.

You can also read the [guide to finding an aesthetic clinic in Malaysia](/guides/the-complete-guide-to-finding-an-aesthetic-clinic-in-malaysia) for a wider shortlist, and [how to compare botox clinics](/guides/botox-clinics-how-to-compare-them) if the procedure you are asking about is botulinum toxin.

Take the doctor's name, the chapter, the expiry, and the written quote from two clinics in the area you will actually visit. The clinic that can put all four in front of you is the one you can compare. The clinic that cannot is still only a sign.
