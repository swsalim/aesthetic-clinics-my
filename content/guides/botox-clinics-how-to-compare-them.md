People searching for botox clinics are usually trying to compare places that inject botulinum toxin. Botox is one brand name for that medicine. In Malaysia it is a prescription medicine, and a registered medical practitioner must administer it. A shopfront that says "botox" is not, by itself, a reason to book.

Use the same three checks at every clinic on your list. Then ask for a written quote. This page does not state a price, a result, or how long a treatment lasts.

## What a botox clinic is offering

The Ministry of Health Malaysia publishes the [Guidelines on Aesthetic Medical Practice for Registered Medical Practitioners](https://hq.moh.gov.my/medicalprac/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Guidelines_on_Aesthetic_Medical_Practice_for_Registered_Medical_Practitioners_2nd_edition.pdf) (second edition, Medical Practice Division, 2020). Chapter 1 of that guideline, written for general practitioners, puts **botulinum toxin injection** on the minimally invasive list. It does not sit with the non-invasive examples, which are superficial chemical peels, microdermabrasion, and intense pulsed light.

That classification matters when you read an advertisement. A clinic can still describe the visit as quick. The guideline's category is the one to ask about: minimally invasive, performed by a registered medical practitioner whose Letter of Credentialing and Privileging (LCP) covers that procedure.

The same guideline says a registered medical practitioner is a person registered under the Medical Act 1971 who holds a valid practising certificate. Ask for the doctor's full name and Malaysian Medical Council (MMC) registration number, and check the [Malaysian Medical Council](https://mmc.gov.my) register. Ask also for the LCP: which chapter, which procedures, and the expiry date. The Medical Practice Division issues the letter. The public lists sit on the Ministry's [National Registry of registered medical practitioners practising aesthetic medical practice](https://www.moh.gov.my/en/core/health-information/national-registry-of-rmp-practising-aesthetic-medical-practice), split into general practitioners, medical specialists, and surgical specialists.

An LCP for a different procedure does not answer the botulinum toxin question. Ask whether the letter names botulinum toxin injection.

## How to compare clinics without comparing slogans

Build a short list, then put the same questions to each clinic. A useful comparison has five lines:

1. **Who injects.** Write the doctor's name, MMC number, and whether you will see that doctor on the day. If a clinic says a nurse or therapist injects, stop and ask for the registered medical practitioner who will perform the procedure.
2. **Which product.** Ask for the product name, not only the word Botox. Brands differ. The quote should name the product the clinic plans to use.
3. **What the quote covers.** Ask which areas the fee includes, whether a review visit is included, and what you would pay if the plan changes after the consultation. Get that in writing. Do not treat a poster price as the quote.
4. **Which LCP chapter applies.** Chapter 1 in the 2020 guideline is for general practitioners and is valid for three years. Chapters 2 and 3, for medical specialists and surgical specialists, are valid for five years. The letter should still be in date.
5. **Where the clinic is.** [Kuala Lumpur](/kuala-lumpur) and [Selangor](/selangor) are separate listing pages. Someone searching for a KL clinic often also looks at [Petaling Jaya](/selangor/petaling-jaya), [Subang Jaya](/selangor/subang-jaya), [Bangsar](/kuala-lumpur/bangsar), [Bukit Bintang](/kuala-lumpur/bukit-bintang), and [Mont Kiara](/kuala-lumpur/mont-kiara). Pick the trip you will actually make, then compare those clinics on the five lines above.

Skip any clinic that will not name the injector or will not put the product on a written quote. Those two facts are the comparison. The fit-out of the waiting room is not.
