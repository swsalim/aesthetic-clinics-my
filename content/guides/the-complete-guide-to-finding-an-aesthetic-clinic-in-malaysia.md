An aesthetic clinic in Malaysia is a place where a registered medical practitioner offers aesthetic medical procedures. The Ministry of Health treats that work as medical practice. The document that says which procedures a doctor may perform is the Letter of Credentialing and Privileging, usually shortened to LCP.

If you are looking for an aesthetic clinic in KL, a skin clinic, or a place for a facial treatment, start with the area you will actually travel to. Then check the doctor. A polished website does not answer either question.

The rules below come from the [Guidelines on Aesthetic Medical Practice for Registered Medical Practitioners](https://hq.moh.gov.my/medicalprac/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Guidelines_on_Aesthetic_Medical_Practice_for_Registered_Medical_Practitioners_2nd_edition.pdf) (second edition, Medical Practice Division, Ministry of Health Malaysia, 2020) and from the Ministry's public registry pages. They describe how to look. They are not a diagnosis and not a recommendation of a procedure.

## How to Compare an Aesthetic Clinic in KL

Kuala Lumpur and Selangor are different listing areas, and people searching for an aesthetic clinic in KL often need both. A clinic in Petaling Jaya is on the [Selangor](/selangor) pages, including [Petaling Jaya](/selangor/petaling-jaya), [Subang Jaya](/selangor/subang-jaya), and [Shah Alam](/selangor/shah-alam). A clinic in the federal territory is on the [Kuala Lumpur](/kuala-lumpur) pages, including [Bukit Bintang](/kuala-lumpur/bukit-bintang), [Bangsar](/kuala-lumpur/bangsar), [Mont Kiara](/kuala-lumpur/mont-kiara), [Cheras](/kuala-lumpur/cheras), [Sri Hartamas](/kuala-lumpur/sri-hartamas), and [Kepong](/kuala-lumpur/kepong).

Compare clinics inside the trip you will make. A Bangsar clinic and a Shah Alam clinic can both be "KL" in casual speech. They are not the same appointment once you count the drive back for a review.

For each clinic on the shortlist, write down six facts:

1. The area page where you found it, so you can get back to the address and phone number.
2. The name of the doctor who will see you, not only the brand on the sign. [Doctor profiles](/doctors) and the state lists at [Kuala Lumpur doctors](/kuala-lumpur/doctors) and [Selangor doctors](/selangor/doctors) are a way to collect names. They are not proof of an LCP.
3. The doctor's Malaysian Medical Council registration number, and what you saw when you checked the [Malaysian Medical Council](https://mmc.gov.my) register.
4. The LCP chapter, the procedures named on the letter, and the expiry date.
5. A written quote that names the procedure, the person who performs it, and what the fee includes. Ask the clinic for the figure. This guide does not publish prices.
6. Whether a review visit is part of that quote, and which branch you would return to.

Use the same six lines for every clinic. A clinic that cannot fill in the doctor, the letter, or the quote is not ready to be compared with one that can.

Location still matters after the paperwork matches. Ask how you will contact the clinic if you have a question the next day, and whether that contact reaches the doctor who treated you. Ask what the consultation includes before anyone books a procedure. You are paying for a medical appointment, so the first visit should be a conversation with the registered practitioner, with time for you to leave and think.

Read recent comments on a listing as comments, not as a scoreboard. This directory does not rank clinics. A handful of reviews cannot tell you whether the doctor holds a current LCP.

## What Makes the Best Aesthetic Clinic in Malaysia?

There is no official list of the best aesthetic clinic in Malaysia, and this directory does not make one. "Best" is a search phrase. The practical version of it is a clinic whose doctor is registered, whose LCP covers the procedure you are asking about, whose premises can explain their licence, and whose quote you can read before you pay.

The 2020 guideline defines aesthetic medical practice, for its own purposes, as an area of practice that uses non-invasive, minimally invasive, and invasive modalities, focused on the skin and underlying structures, carried out by registered medical practitioners. It also says aesthetic medical practice is not recognised as a medical specialty in Malaysia. It is an area of interest inside medical practice, and the practitioners remain under the Malaysian Medical Council's Code of Professional Conduct and the other laws that govern practice.

That is why a national "best clinic" award on a homepage is the wrong sorting tool. Sort by the procedure. A general practitioner and a dermatologist and a plastic surgeon are credentialed through different chapters of the same guideline. The best match is the chapter that fits the procedure, not the loudest claim.

Look at four things that actually change the visit:

- **The practitioner.** The person who performs the procedure should be the person on the LCP. Ask if anyone else will inject, operate a device, or do a peel, and what that person is allowed to do.
- **The procedure category.** Ask the clinic to name the treatment the way the guideline does: non-invasive, minimally invasive, or invasive. Then ask which line of the relevant chapter it falls under.
- **The premises.** The guideline says practitioners should provide these services in a facility licensed or registered under the Private Healthcare Facilities and Services Act 1998 and the related 2006 regulations. Ask which licence applies to the address you will visit.
- **The quote.** Fees move with the procedure, the product, the number of areas, the doctor's chapter, and whether follow-up is included. Get those items in writing from the clinic. Ignore any number you did not get from them.

If you want a second name to compare, take the same procedure to a second clinic in the same area and ask for the same four items. Two complete answers beat ten incomplete ones.

The [Medical Practice Division page on aesthetic practice](https://hq.moh.gov.my/medicalprac/estetik/) tells the public to check the procedure and the practitioner before going ahead: the procedure's category, and a registered medical practitioner who holds an LCP, confirmed with the MMC. Put that check first. Comfort, parking, and package names come after it.

## How to Verify an LCP Certified Aesthetic Clinic

LCP stands for Letter of Credentialing and Privileging. The Medical Practice Division issues it on the recommendation of the Main Credentialing and Privileging Committee of Aesthetic Medical Practice. The letter follows training and assessment. It is not a sticker the clinic designs for itself.

The national registry has three chapters, and the Ministry publishes them as separate lists:

- Chapter 1, general practitioners. In the 2020 guideline this letter is valid for three years.
- Chapter 2, medical specialists. This letter is valid for five years.
- Chapter 3, surgical specialists. This letter is valid for five years.

Each can be renewed. The public files are on the [National Registry of registered medical practitioners practising aesthetic medical practice](https://www.moh.gov.my/en/core/health-information/national-registry-of-rmp-practising-aesthetic-medical-practice). Download the current chapter list rather than trusting a screenshot in a brochure.

Verify in this order:

1. Ask the clinic for the doctor's full name, MMC number, LCP chapter, the procedures printed on the letter, and the expiry date. Ask for a copy or a clear photograph of the letter.
2. Search the Malaysian Medical Council register and confirm the doctor is registered and holds a valid practising certificate. The guideline's definition of a registered medical practitioner is a person registered and holding a valid practising certificate under the Medical Act 1971.
3. Find the same doctor on the Ministry registry chapter that matches the letter. A Chapter 1 list does not confirm a specialist letter, and the reverse is also true.
4. Match the procedure. The guideline requires an LCP for the procedure the doctor intends to perform. A letter for lasers does not answer a question about fillers. A letter for fillers does not answer a question about surgery.
5. If the clinic has several branches, ask which doctor will be at the branch you are booking. The LCP is the doctor's, not the brand's.

## Choosing Facial Treatment in Malaysia

"Facial treatment" covers a wide set of visits, from a surface peel to an injection to a laser. The guideline sorts aesthetic medical procedures into non-invasive, minimally invasive, and invasive. Choosing a treatment starts by asking which of those three you are being offered, and who is allowed to do it.

For general practitioners, Chapter 1 lists the non-invasive procedures as superficial chemical peels, microdermabrasion, and intense pulsed light. The minimally invasive list includes medium-depth chemical peels, botulinum toxin injection, filler injection excluding silicone and fat, skin-tightening procedures up to the upper dermis, superficial sclerotherapy, and lasers for pigmentation, benign skin lesions, rejuvenation, and hair removal. That is the GP list in the guideline. The full table, including the training note, is in the PDF. Ask the clinic to point at the row that matches the treatment they proposed.

Specialist chapters cover a wider scope for doctors who qualify for them. If a clinic recommends a procedure that is outside the GP list, ask which specialist will perform it and which chapter their LCP uses. Do not accept "our therapist is very experienced" as a substitute for that name.

When you enquire, send a short note so the replies are comparable:

- The concern you want assessed, in your own words. You are asking for an assessment, not naming your own procedure.
- The area of the clinic you will visit, for example [Mont Kiara](/kuala-lumpur/mont-kiara) or [Puchong](/selangor/puchong).
- A request for the doctor's name, MMC number, and the LCP row for the procedure they would consider.
- A request for a written quote after the consultation, with the product name if a product is used.

Compare the replies. A clinic that names a device brand and will not name the doctor has answered the wrong question. A clinic that tells you the procedure category, the doctor, and what the quote will include has given you something you can take to a second clinic.

Botulinum toxin is a common facial enquiry and a common source of confusion, because advertisements call it Botox. Botox is a brand. The medicine is botulinum toxin, it is a prescription medicine, and a registered medical practitioner must administer it. The guideline places the injection on the minimally invasive list. The separate note on [comparing botox clinics](/guides/botox-clinics-how-to-compare-them) covers the questions to ask about the product and the injector.

Other notes on this site cover [chemical peel treatment](/guides/chemical-peel-treatment-malaysia), [dermaplane treatment](/guides/dermaplane-treatment) and [melasma treatment](/guides/melasma-treatment-malaysia). Each one is general information about what to ask and what to check. None of them recommends a procedure or a clinic.

Prices for facial treatments are not listed here. They change with the procedure, the product, the areas treated, who performs it, and the follow-up. Ask each clinic for a written quote. If a promotion is on a poster, ask for that promotion in the same document, including what happens if you do not continue a package.

## When to Consider Non Invasive Aesthetic Treatments

Consider a non-invasive procedure when the doctor, after an assessment, proposes one of the treatments the guideline actually calls non-invasive, and when that doctor's LCP covers it. For a general practitioner, the 2020 Chapter 1 examples are superficial chemical peels, microdermabrasion, and intense pulsed light. The definition in the guideline is an external application or treatment with no break in the skin, targeting the epidermis only.

That is a narrower meaning than the phrase "non invasive aesthetic treatments" in advertising. Injections are not in that group. The guideline lists botulinum toxin and filler injections as minimally invasive. Lasers on the GP list are minimally invasive as well. If a clinic calls an injection non-invasive, ask them to show you where the guideline puts it. You want the category and the LCP row, not a softer adjective.

A few practical filters, still short of medical advice:

- You have a named doctor and a current letter for that class of procedure.
- You understand whether the plan is a single visit or a series, because the quote should say so.
- You know which address you must return to if the clinic has more than one branch. [Cheras](/kuala-lumpur/cheras), [Kajang](/selangor/kajang), and [Klang](/selangor/klang) are different trips.
- You have time to read the consent form. The guideline tells practitioners to communicate risks and obtain valid consent. A non-invasive label does not remove that conversation. The guideline also says aesthetic procedures are not completely safe.

You do not have to book at the first consultation. Ask for the written plan and the quote, then compare them with one other clinic on your shortlist. If you are unsure which category a treatment falls into, ask the clinic to show you the page of the guideline they are using. You can also read that page yourself.

Invasive procedures are a different decision. They sit with the doctors credentialed for them, often through Chapter 3 for surgical specialists. This guide will not walk through surgical choices. If a clinic proposes an invasive procedure, ask for the specialist's name, their National Specialist Register status where the guideline requires it, and the procedures on their LCP, and take those to the consultation.

## Aesthetic Clinic or Skin Clinic Malaysia: Which Should You Choose?

Clinics use both names. An "aesthetic clinic" and a "skin clinic" can be the same medical practice, a dermatology practice, or a beauty salon that does not provide aesthetic medical procedures at all. The name on the glass is not the thing you choose. You choose the person who will perform the specific procedure, and you check that they are allowed to perform it.

Use the labels this way:

- If the visit is an aesthetic medical procedure in the Ministry guideline, you want a registered medical practitioner with an LCP that names that procedure. The setting may call itself a skin clinic, an aesthetic clinic, or a medispa. Ask for the letter anyway.
- If the doctor is a dermatologist or another medical specialist, ask whether they are on the Chapter 2 registry and which procedures their letter covers. Dermatology training and an LCP are related checks, not identical ones. The guideline sets out the specialist prerequisites, including Malaysian Medical Council registration and a current Annual Practising Certificate.
- If the visit is a salon facial with no medical procedure, the LCP framework is the wrong yardstick, and this directory is the wrong shortlist. This site lists aesthetic clinics and aesthetic doctors, not beauty salons.

A useful way to sort a skin clinic Malaysia search is to ignore the title and ask one question in the first message: "Which registered medical practitioner would see me, and which LCP procedures are you proposing?" The reply will tell you whether you are in a medical clinic.

Then place them on a map you can use. [Kuala Lumpur](/kuala-lumpur) and [Selangor](/selangor) are the largest clusters on this directory. Other states are on the [browse page](/browse). Open two area pages you can reach, collect two or three clinics, and run the doctor, letter, and quote check on each. Keep the clinic that can show you those three documents for the procedure you discussed.

That is the whole task. A shortlist from the area pages, a doctor you can find on the MMC register, an LCP that names the procedure and has not expired, and a written quote from the clinic. Book the appointment only after those four are in front of you.
