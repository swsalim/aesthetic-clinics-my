Brown or grey-brown patches can appear gradually across the cheeks, forehead, nose or upper lip, then become more noticeable after time outdoors. Choosing a cream or laser package before confirming the diagnosis can leave you with irritated skin and darker marks. A safer starting point for melasma treatment is a medical assessment, followed by a plan that matches your skin, history and tolerance.

**Quick answer:** Melasma is a chronic pigmentation condition that often needs a combination of sun protection, prescribed treatment and careful follow-up. A dermatologist or qualified aesthetic doctor should confirm the diagnosis before you use prescription products, chemical peels, laser or light-based procedures.

## What Is Melasma?

Melasma is an acquired condition involving increased pigmentation. It usually appears as fairly flat brown or grey-brown patches on areas exposed to light, especially the cheeks, forehead, bridge of the nose and upper lip. The patches often appear on both sides of the face, although appearance alone cannot confirm the diagnosis.

Several other skin conditions can resemble melasma. A doctor may need to distinguish it from post-inflammatory pigmentation, medication-related pigmentation or another lesion that needs separate care. A photograph or online description cannot make that distinction reliably.

Sun exposure can deepen the colour, and visible light may also affect pigmentation in some people. Hormonal factors can matter too, including pregnancy or hormonal medicines. Malaysia's strong year-round sunlight makes daily protection a practical part of any treatment plan, even on days spent mostly indoors.

Melasma often returns after it improves. The American Journal of Clinical Dermatology describes it as a chronic acquired disorder that can be difficult to manage over the medium and long term. Research reviews also discuss treatment combinations because several processes can contribute to pigmentation. Read the [2024 treatment review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40257-024-00863-2) for the clinical background.

Melasma treatment can reduce visible pigmentation, but it cannot guarantee permanent clearance.

## How Is Melasma Assessed Before Treatment?

A consultation prevents the common mistake of treating every dark patch as melasma. The doctor will usually examine the distribution, colour and surface of the pigmentation and ask how it started or changed.

Prepare to discuss:

- Previous skin conditions, allergies and medical diagnoses.
- Current skincare products, prescription medicines and supplements.
- Pregnancy, plans for pregnancy or hormonal medicines where relevant.
- Sun exposure, outdoor work, heat exposure and use of sunscreen.
- Past peels, lasers, light treatments or products that caused burning or peeling.

The doctor may also ask whether the patches itch, bleed, crust, become raised or change quickly. Those features do not establish a diagnosis, but they deserve medical review rather than self-treatment. Seek assessment for sudden, changing or unusual pigmentation, especially if one spot looks different from the others.

Bring the products you use, or clear photographs of their ingredient lists. In Malaysia, this matters because the National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency states that products containing hydroquinone or tretinoin are pharmaceutical products requiring registration and professional advice. Its warning also describes redness, discomfort, discolouration and hypersensitivity linked to unsupervised use of adulterated products. Read the [NPRA statement](https://www.npra.gov.my/index.php/en/component/content/article/215-english/press-release/press-release-2018/1756-press-statement-director-general-of-health-cosmetic-products-found-to-contain-scheduled-poison) before buying an unverified lightening product online.

### When not to self-treat

- When pigmentation appeared suddenly or is changing quickly.
- When a patch bleeds, crusts, hurts, itches persistently or becomes raised.
- When pregnancy, breastfeeding or a new medicine may affect treatment choices.
- When previous products caused swelling, blistering or marked irritation.

## Melasma Treatment Options a Doctor May Discuss

The right option depends on the diagnosis, depth and extent of pigmentation, skin type, previous treatment and your risk of irritation. A doctor may recommend one approach first, then adjust it after reviewing how your skin responds.

### Prescription topical treatments

A doctor may discuss prescription creams or combinations that reduce pigment production or speed the removal of pigmented cells. The active ingredient, strength, application schedule and duration need medical direction. Using more product will not necessarily produce faster improvement and can trigger irritation.

### Chemical peels

A chemical peel applies a controlled solution to the skin. The solution, concentration, contact time and aftercare affect the risk. Peels can cause burning, prolonged redness or post-inflammatory pigmentation, particularly when the skin barrier is already irritated.

A separate note, [chemical peel treatment in Malaysia](/guides/chemical-peel-treatment-malaysia), covers how to compare peel clinics and what to ask about formulation, aftercare and fees.

### Laser and light-based procedures

Laser and light devices may be discussed for selected patients, but they are not suitable for everyone with melasma. Heat or inflammation can worsen pigmentation, especially in darker skin tones or after an incorrect setting. A clinic should explain the device, settings in broad terms, expected downtime and what happens if pigmentation worsens.

### Supportive skincare and sun protection

Gentle cleansing, moisturising and consistent broad-spectrum sun protection can support a treatment plan. Your doctor may discuss protection from visible light as well as ultraviolet exposure. Stop and report persistent burning, swelling, blistering or rapidly darkening skin instead of adding another active product.

A 2024 review in the [American Journal of Clinical Dermatology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40257-024-00863-2) discusses topical agents, visible-light photoprotection, chemical peels, oral tranexamic acid and energy-based devices. The paper does not turn those options into a universal prescription. Your doctor must decide whether an option fits your circumstances.

| Option | What to ask | Possible concern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prescription topical treatment | Which ingredient, schedule and review date apply? | Irritation or inappropriate unsupervised use |
| Chemical peel | What solution, preparation and aftercare are planned? | Burning or post-inflammatory pigmentation |
| Laser or light procedure | Why is it suitable for my skin and diagnosis? | Worsened pigmentation or downtime |
| Supportive skincare | Which products should be paused or continued? | Barrier damage from too many active products |

For melasma, a gentler plan that your skin tolerates is often more useful than an aggressive procedure you cannot maintain.

## What to Know About Safety, Recurrence and Expectations

Melasma treatment usually aims to reduce contrast and prevent worsening, rather than promise a permanent change. Patients respond differently because triggers, pigmentation depth, skin sensitivity, hormonal factors and previous irritation differ.

Possible problems include dryness, stinging, redness, peeling, allergic reactions, post-inflammatory pigmentation and recurrence. Procedures add risks related to heat, inflammation, infection and prolonged colour changes. Ask which symptoms are expected after treatment and which require an urgent call to the clinic.

Maintenance may include sun protection, a prescribed topical plan or periodic review. Do not restart an old prescription without checking with the treating doctor, especially after pregnancy, a new diagnosis or a change in medication.

For example, a patient whose patches darken after a peel may need the doctor to pause active products, assess inflammation and revise the plan. Repeating the same peel immediately could worsen the problem. The safest next step depends on examination, not a package schedule.

Follow the aftercare instructions supplied by your doctor. Report severe pain, blistering, facial swelling, signs of infection or rapidly worsening pigmentation. A 2025 network meta-analysis in [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41149049/) compares evidence across treatments, but study findings still require clinical interpretation for an individual patient.

## How to Choose a Clinic for Melasma Treatment in Malaysia

Clinic comparison should begin with the doctor and diagnosis, not with the lowest advertised package. A directory can help you identify clinics and doctors in a city or state, while the consultation determines whether the proposed care suits you.

Use Aesthetic Clinics MY to [browse aesthetic clinics in Malaysia](/browse) and [find aesthetic doctors in Malaysia](/doctors). Listings, reviews and displayed prices can help you compare options, but they do not replace an examination or confirm that a procedure is appropriate.

Before booking, ask these questions:

- Who will assess my pigmentation, and what qualifications and relevant experience do they have?
- What diagnosis is being considered, and what other conditions have been ruled out?
- Why is this treatment suitable for my skin type and history?
- How many sessions might be considered, and what downtime is possible?
- What are the risks, alternatives and signs of a bad reaction?
- What aftercare, review appointments and maintenance might be needed?
- What is the total fee, including consultation, medication, follow-up and additional sessions?

Ask the clinic to identify the person performing a procedure and explain who handles complications. In Malaysia, the Ministry of Health's [Guidelines on Aesthetic Medical Practice](https://hq.moh.gov.my/medicalprac/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Guidelines_on_Aesthetic_Medical_Practice_for_Registered_Medical_Practitioners_2nd_edition.pdf) provide official context for registered medical practitioners and aesthetic practice. Check current MOH information directly because regulatory requirements can change.

The term LCP may appear in Malaysian aesthetic clinic information. Ask the clinic what the doctor's LCP status covers and how it relates to the proposed procedure. A listing or the phrase alone should not replace checking the doctor's identity, consultation process and treatment explanation.
